Stamp a document

Your key is made and kept on this device, or stays offline on your own computer. The document is read here and never uploaded. The seal records the time between two Bitcoin blocks.

1Your seal

Your seal is a key and the artwork in its center. Make one here, use a key you keep offline, or open a key file you saved before.

This is how your seal will look. The dots in the ring change with every document.

Your private key stays on your own computer. This page needs only your public key, and asks you to sign a short code whenever it needs a signature.

Sign this code with your offline key. It is the SHA-256 hash (32 bytes) of the record that ties your artwork to your key.

With the DigiHan Python tools: python sign_hash.py CODE --key YOUR_KEY. Any BIP340 signer works.

Save the key file and keep the password. With both you can stamp on any device. This page keeps the key file on this device, encrypted with your password.

2Your document

Any file: a PDF, a picture, a spreadsheet. You get back a PDF with the seal on its first page and your file attached unchanged.

    Sign this code with your offline key, then paste the signature. The code is one SHA-256 hash (32 bytes) made from this document's hash, the time, your key's fingerprint, your artwork's hash and, if chosen, the Bitcoin block's hash.

    Download the stamped PDF

    To check it: open the scanner, scan the seal, then tap “Check against a document…” and pick this PDF.

    3Finish the time proof

    A few hours after stamping, pick the stamped PDF. The page fetches the Bitcoin part of the proof and gives you the finished PDF.
